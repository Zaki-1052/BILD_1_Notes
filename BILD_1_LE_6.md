## Concentration Gradients

### Understanding Movement

- **High to Low Concentration**
  - Down/with the concentration gradient
  - Movement occurs naturally in this direction
  - No energy required

- **Low to High Concentration**
  - Up/against the concentration gradient
  - Requires energy input
  - Movement must be actively driven

### Movement of Water (Osmosis)

#### Relative to Outside Solution

1. **When Solution Outside is Extremely Salty**
   - Water moves out of cell
   - Cell shrinks

2. **When Solution Outside is Pure Water (or Gatorade)**
   - Water moves into cell
   - Cell swells

3. **When Solution Outside Matches Inside Concentration**
   - No net water movement
   - Cell maintains normal size

#### Gibbs Free Energy (G)

- **Definition**: The energy that a molecule holds in its bonds
- Most common way to quantify energy in biochemical reactions
- Units: kJ/mol or kcal/mol
- Breaking bonds releases energy
	- Reactants/Products each have a G, and you can sum them together to get a total G.

#### ΔG (Delta G)
- Calculated as: ΔG = G(products) - G(reactants)
- Tells us about reaction spontaneity
- Does NOT depend on activation energy

### Types of Reactions

#### Exergonic Reactions (Spontaneous)
- ΔG is negative
- Reaction gives off energy
- Can happen by itself
- Examples:
  - Ice melting at room temperature
  - Breaking down carbohydrates
  - Ions moving down concentration gradients
  - ATP breakdown to ADP + Phosphate (ΔG = -7.3 kcal/mol)

#### Endergonic Reactions (Non-spontaneous)
- ΔG is positive
- Reaction absorbs energy
- Won't happen without energy input
- Examples:
  - Making glucose
  - Making DNA
  - Making proteins
  - Moving things against concentration gradients

### The Activation Energy "Hump"

- Energy needed initially to start a reaction
- Like striking a match
- Called "activation energy"
- Present in both exergonic and endergonic reactions
- Does not affect overall ΔG

## ATP and Energy Coupling

### ATP as Energy Currency

- **ATP** (Adenosine Triphosphate) is the main energy carrier
- Key reaction: ATP → ADP + Phosphate
  - ΔG = -7.3 kcal/mol
  - Releases energy that can power other reactions
  - Exergonic reaction

### Example of Coupled Reactions: Glutamine Synthesis

#### Initial Reaction (Endergonic)
- Glutamate + NH3 → Glutamine
- ΔG = +3.4 kcal/mol
- Not spontaneous on its own

#### How ATP Helps

2. **Step 1: ATP Phosphorylation**
   - ATP dissociates and transfers phosphate to glutamate
   - Creates high-energy phosphorylated intermediate
   - ADP is released

3. **Step 2: Glutamine Formation**
   - Phosphorylated glutamate reacts with ammonia
   - Forms glutamine
   - Phosphate is released
   - Energy is provided for the reaction

#### Net Effect
- Combined ΔG values:
  - Glutamine formation: +3.4 kcal/mol
  - ATP breakdown: -7.3 kcal/mol
  - Net ΔG = -3.9 kcal/mol
- Overall reaction becomes exergonic!

### Key Principles

4. **Energy Coupling**
   - Exergonic reactions can power endergonic ones
   - ATP breakdown often provides the energy
   - Must be physically linked through shared intermediates

5. **Phosphorylation**
   - Adding phosphate group from ATP
   - Creates high-energy intermediate
   - Common mechanism in cellular reactions

6. **Conservation of Energy**
   - Energy is neither created nor destroyed
   - Only converted between forms
   - Some energy is lost as heat

## Applications and Examples

### Diamond to Graphite Conversion
- Has negative ΔG (thermodynamically favorable)
- Is spontaneous in theory
- Very high activation energy barrier
- Explains why diamonds are stable in practice

### Na+/K+ Pump Operation
- Moves ions against concentration gradients
- Requires ATP phosphorylation
- Example of endergonic process coupled to ATP hydrolysis

### Questions

1. **Concentration Gradients**
   - Movement from high to low is spontaneous
   - Movement from low to high requires energy
   - Na+/K+ pump mechanisms require ATP

2. **Transport Types**
   - Passive: No energy needed (down gradient)
   - Active: Requires energy (against gradient)
   - Bulk transport for large molecules

3. **Hydrophobic vs Hydrophilic**
   - Hydrophobic molecules pass easily through membrane
   - Hydrophilic molecules need help from proteins
   - Charged molecules have most difficulty

4. **Equilibrium and Water**
   - Equilibrium = no net movement
   - Osmosis = water movement
   - Can involve other molecules besides water
