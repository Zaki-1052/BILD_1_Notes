### Historical Results

- If the mass of the tree had come from the soil, the soil would have much less mass.
- But the soil only lost 2oz.
- → Soil is not the source of most of the tree's mass.
- Soil is important and does contribute some minerals, but this is not the majority of the mass.

### Conservation of Mass and Energy

If we knew nothing about plants, which choice could we rule out using conservation of mass and energy?

A. Absorption of mineral substances from the soil via roots
B. Absorption of organic substances from the soil via roots.
C. Incorporation of carbon dioxide gas from the atmosphere into molecules by green leaves.
D. Incorporation of water from the soil into molecules by green leaves.
E. Absorption of sunlight energy into green leaves.

> Sunlight energy has no mass and can't create mass.

### Big Idea: Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis captures energy from sunlight and stores it in the chemical bond energy of glucose, which is built from CO2.

CO2 + H2O → Glucose + O2
Light energy

Mass transformed into mass:
CO2 & H2O to Glucose & O2

Energy transformed into energy:
Sunlight Energy to Chemical Bond Energy

### What happens to the glucose

- The plant uses some of the glucose to make ATP to power cellular reactions (cellular respiration).
- The plant uses some of it to make its own body's biological molecules, like cellulose in wood, to grow and replenish its tissues.
	- Mass comes mostly from the glucose
	- CO2 + H2O → Glucose + O2
		- Light energy

### Source of Atoms in Glucose

#### What is the source of the carbon atoms in glucose?

A. Carbon dioxide
B. Water
C. Oxygen
D. Sunlight

Light energy
CO2 + H2O → C6H12O6 + O2
(Balanced equation:
6CO2 + 6H2O → C6H12O6 + 6O2)

> Carbon makes up a lot of the dry biomass.

#### What is the source of the hydrogen atoms in glucose?

A. Carbon dioxide
B. Water
C. Oxygen
D. Sunlight

Light energy
CO2 + H2O → C6H12O6 + O2

> Hydrogen is very light and does NOT contribute much to the dry biomass of a tree.

### Source of the oxygen atoms in glucose

Questions to answer:
- Do the oxygen atoms in glucose come from the carbon dioxide or the water?
- Do the oxygen atoms in the oxygen gas come from the carbon dioxide or the water?

Light energy
CO2 + H2O → C6H12O6 + O2

### Isotopes: Not all oxygen atoms are the same

- Typical oxygen: 8 neutrons: 16O
- Heavy oxygen: 10 neutrons: 18O
- Same bonds and chemistry as normal 16O

### Question: Where does the oxygen atom in water end up?

- They gave plants:
	- Water with heavy oxygen (H218O)
	- Carbon dioxide with normal oxygen (CO2)
- They found:
	- The plants released oxygen gas released with heavy oxygen (18O2)

### What can you definitely conclude from this experiment?

A. The oxygen atoms in water can become part of oxygen gas.
B. The oxygen atoms in carbon dioxide become part of glucose.
C. Both A and B
D. Neither A nor B

Based on this experiment alone, we can't say for sure what the oxygen in carbon dioxide does.

### Question: Where do the oxygen atoms in carbon dioxide end up?

- Gave plants:
	- Water with normal oxygen (H2O)
	- Carbon dioxide with heavy oxygen (C18O2)
- They found:
	- None of the oxygen gas released had heavy oxygen

### What can you conclude from these two experiments together?

A. The oxygen atoms in water can become part of oxygen gas.
B. The oxygen atoms in carbon dioxide become part of glucose.
C. Both A and B
D. Neither A nor B

If the oxygen atoms in carbon dioxide don't go in the oxygen gas, they must go into the glucose.

### Where do the oxygen atoms in glucose come from?

A. The carbon dioxide only
B. The water only
C. The oxygen gas only

Light energy
CO2 + H2O → C6H12O6 + O2

The oxygen in water becomes part of oxygen gas, and the oxygen in carbon dioxide becomes part of glucose.

### Does photosynthesis "turn CO2 into O2"?

No:
- This would not conserve mass (because of the carbon)
- O2 is generated from the water, not the CO2
	- O2 is generated and CO2 is used up, but in totally different processes
	- O2 is generated "first"
