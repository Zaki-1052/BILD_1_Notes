#### Chlorophyll and photosynthesis
- These are the needles of a "ghost" redwood tree, whose leaves are all white. 
- Does this tree do photosynthesis?
	- C. No. If the entire plant is is white, it cannot do photosynthesis. It lacks the green compound chlorophyll, which is found in chloroplasts and needed for photosynthesis.
### Two Stages of Photosynthesis

- Light reactions turn solar energy into energy in chemical bonds
	- Sunlight activates the chlorophyll in the chloroplast's photosystems 
	- Photosystems generate ATP from ADP and NADPH from NADP+
	- As they do so, they split water to release O2
- The Calvin cycle uses the ATP and NADPH from light reactions to turn CO2 into sugar
	- Uses enzyme RuBisCo
	- Puts CO2 into organic molecules (carbon fixation)

### Light Reactions

### Calvin Cycle

![[calvinCycle.png]]

1. The enzyme RuBisCo adds CO2 to a certain molecule, RuBP.
2. Enzymes chemically modify and split the molecule.
3. RuBP is regenerated.

#### Calvin Cycle: What are the...

• Inputs in terms of energy? Chemical bond energy in ATP and NADPH
• Outputs in terms of energy? Chemical bond energy in sugars
• Inputs in terms of mass? ATP, NADPH, CO2
• Outputs in terms of mass? ADP + Pi, NADP+, sugars

#### What part of photosynthesis do plants do at night?
A. All of it
B. Calvin cycle (the "dark reactions" of photosynthesis) only
C. None of it
- C. None of it
	- The light reactions require there to be light immediately available. The Calvin cycle doesn't.
	- But the Calvin cycle depends on the ATP and the NADPH the light reactions make. So if the light reactions aren't happening, the Calvin cycle will quickly stop.

### Big Idea: Cellular Respiration

Cellular respiration transforms energy from the chemical bonds of food into energy in the chemical bonds of ATP.

Glucose + 6O2 → 6CO2 + 6H2O 

#### Mass transformed into mass:
- Glucose & O2 to CO2 & H2O

#### Energy transformed into energy:
- Bonds of Glucose to Bonds of ATP
- Pi + ADP → ATP

#### Why we use ATP instead of keeping it as carbohydrates

ATP can be used to directly power many cellular reactions

• Energy in sugar is like a check someone has written you, while energy in ATP is like a $20 bill

#### ATP Formation

#### Is this reaction endergonic or exergonic?

A. Endergonic
B. Exergonic
C. Not enough information to tell

![[atpFormation.png]]

#### Answer:
A. Endergonic. If the reverse reaction (ATP → ADP + Pi) is exergonic, ATP must have more energy than ADP + Pi. So, ADP + Pi → ATP must be endergonic.
