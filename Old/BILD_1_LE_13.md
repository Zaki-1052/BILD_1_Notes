- **Hydrophilic signals**:
	- Various cellular responses:
		- Enzyme activation
		- Release of ions
		- Eventually gene expression

### Receptor Tyrosine Kinases

- There are many different types of receptors
- To understand how a receptor can work, we will be exploring a specific class of receptors:
	- The receptor tyrosine kinases (RTKs)
		- Important in signaling growth, hormone signaling, insulin signaling, and many other roles
		- Can be important in cancers

#### Structure and Function
- They are receptors
- They contain amino acids called tyrosine
- They are kinases: proteins that transfer phosphate groups from ATP to another protein
	- Process called phosphorylation
	- It can activate enzymes

![[receptorTyrosineKinase.png]]

### RTK Activation

#### How receptor tyrosine kinases are activated:

1. **Signal Binding and Dimerization**
2. **Phosphorylation, Activation, and Transduction**
3. (not shown) Signal & phosphates leave, activation ends

##### Process:
4. Only when the ligand is present, two of the RTKs dimerize (pair up)
5. They phosphorylate each other → activating each other
6. Now, they can activate other proteins to start signal transduction

![[rtkActivation.png]]

### Phosphorylation Cascades

1. Signal/ligand
2. Receptor
3. Signal transduction pathways
4. Cell's response

#### Box: Phosphorylation Cascade
- Kinase phosphorylating another kinase, which phosphorylates another kinase, etc.
- **KEY FACT**: Each kinase can phosphorylate about 10 targets before it loses activation

##### Example Numbers:
- Active Raf (100)
- Active MEK (1,000)
- Active ERK (10,000)

#### Question
- Say that this phosphorylation cascade is activated. What molecule will be most abundant?
	- A. Active Raf
	- B. Active MEK
	- C. Active ERK
	- D. They will all be present in the same amounts

##### Answer
C. Active ERK
- At each stage, each active molecule can activate a bunch of other molecules, which really adds up!

### Drug Development Question

#### Question
- If you were creating a drug to inhibit the response, which molecule would you target?

A. Molecules near the beginning of the cascade
B. Molecules near the middle of the cascade  
C. Molecules near the end of the cascade

##### Answer
A. Molecules near the beginning of the cascade
- Imagine that each molecule of your drug inhibits 1 enzyme
- Your drug will have more effect at lower dose if you target the beginning of the cascade

### Central Dogma and Traits

#### How the instructions in DNA (genes) relate to traits

##### Central Dogma:
DNA (gene) → RNA → Protein - - -> Trait

- Process is Transcription (DNA to RNA)
- Process is Translation (RNA to Protein)
- A gene is a stretch of DNA that codes for a protein

![[centralDogma.png]]

### Genes as Blueprints

- Genes (Nucleic Acids) are like blueprints
	- DNA: blueprints
	- Protein: A building element like a beam of wood
	- Trait: a flat roof built out of the wood

![[geneBlueprint.png]]

#### Important Concept
- If we change the blueprints (genes), the parts of the house (traits) change too
- But it's indirect - the protein is the step in between
